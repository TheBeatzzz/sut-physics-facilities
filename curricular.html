B.Sc. Physics / Draft 2570

Undergraduate curriculum
at a glance.

Based on the revised B.Sc. Physics draft curriculum, students move from physics, mathematics, laboratory work, programming, and scientific communication toward specialized electives, senior project or cooperative education, and pathways into research or technology careers.

Credits157

Minimum credits across general education, science core, physics major courses, electives, project or co-op, and free electives.

SystemTrimester

Three study terms per academic year: June-September, October-January, and February-May.

Study modeOn campus

Learning uses SUT classrooms, laboratories, library and computing resources, equipment centers, and approved work-integrated sites.

Estimated fee41,800

Approximate education fee per year in the draft document, with final payment rules confirmed by the university.

What students learn to do

  • Use physics principles and mathematical models to explain real phenomena.
  • Design experiments, analyze data, quantify uncertainty, and use computational tools.
  • Apply physics in advanced areas such as materials, astrophysics, photonics, quantum technology, or medical physics.
  • Communicate scientific work clearly and use digital technology and AI responsibly.

Specialized directions

  • Computation, Data and AI
  • Waves, Optics and Photonics
  • Quantum Information Science and Technology
  • Condensed Matter and Materials
  • Spectroscopy, Synchrotron, Radiation and Particle Physics
  • Interdisciplinary Applied Sciences and Physics Education

Career pathways

  • Researcher, scientist, physicist, or technical specialist in research institutes and laboratories.
  • Industry roles in electronics, semiconductors, photonics, telecommunications, sensors, testing, calibration, and quality control.
  • Data analyst, modeling or simulation developer, and quantitative analyst in data-intensive organizations.
  • Teacher, academic staff, science communicator, media creator, or STEM learning professional.

Admissions note Applicants should have completed upper secondary education or an equivalent recognized qualification, with mathematics and science foundations suitable for physics study.

03 / Learning areas

Skills for
physics futures.

As you study physics at SUT, you can build scientific thinking, laboratory confidence, computational habits, and communication skills that support both research and industry pathways.

01

Think like a physicist

Learn to connect mathematics, computation, and theory with questions about matter, energy, fields, motion, and complex systems.

02

Work with real evidence

Practice experiments, instrumentation, uncertainty, and data analysis so observations become reliable scientific evidence.

03

Find a research direction

Discover how classroom ideas connect with faculty expertise, facilities, projects, and the research questions you may want to pursue.

04

Tell the story of your work

Develop reports, presentations, thesis or dissertation writing, and research communication for academic and professional audiences.

04 / Study journey

How your study
can progress.

From foundational coursework to research outputs, the curriculum gives students a clearer path for growing into independent study, thesis work, and professional readiness.

  1. All levels

    Start with a strong foundation

    Core study builds the mathematical, conceptual, experimental, and professional base that helps students move confidently into advanced topics.

  2. Master

    Prepare for deeper study

    Master's study helps students consolidate advanced knowledge before focusing more fully on a thesis question or applied research problem.

  3. PhD

    Grow toward independent research

    Doctoral study develops the readiness to define original questions, choose appropriate methods, and contribute new knowledge.

  4. Master · PhD

    Shape a research project

    Graduate students move from an initial idea toward a proposal, guided research, and thesis or dissertation work with faculty support.

  5. Master · PhD

    Share work with the wider community

    Research communication, publication preparation, and final defense help students present their work to academic and professional audiences.
